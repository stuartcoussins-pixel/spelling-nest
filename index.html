Why it works

Not a spelling test. A week of small, useful practice.

Most spelling lists get a quick read the night before and a test a few days later. Spelling Nest spreads the same list across the whole week and keeps score of the only thing that matters: which words your child can now spell without help.

Spelling from sound, not copying

Copying a word off the page is easy to do and easy to forget. In Spelling Nest your child hears the word and builds it from letter tiles, which is much closer to what a test asks of them.

Mastered means mastered

A word only counts as mastered once it has been spelled from sound, first time, with no clues, on three different days. One lucky guess does not clear it off the list.

Help arrives before the tears do

If your child is stuck, Kit the owl replays the word slowly with each letter group lighting up, then offers the first letter, then the number of letters and the tricky part. Nobody sits there defeated.

Alongside the writing, not instead of it

Spelling Nest does not replace the exercise book, and it is not meant to. It is the same look, cover, write, check your child's school uses, with enough pull that the practice actually happens. Keep writing the words out by hand as well.

How it works

Three steps, once a week.

1

A grown up adds the week's words

Type or paste the list that came home from school. Spelling Nest tidies it up, drops duplicates and shows each word as a chip you can remove. Add the day of the test and your child sees a friendly countdown.

  • Any list works, because it is your school's list
  • Saved lists can be reused, edited or deleted
  • One account can hold several children, each with their own words and progress
2

Your child plays a short lesson

A lesson is six to twelve questions and usually takes under ten minutes. Every word comes up at least once, and the words that are not yet safe come up more often. Answers are always given by tapping letter tiles, so there is no keyboard to fight with.

  • Five hearts a lesson, and a missed word returns later as a gentler question
  • Hear it and Slowly buttons on every question
  • XP, day streaks and ten badges to collect
3

You see what has stuck

Every lesson ends with a scorecard: how many words were right first time, which ones need more work, and any words newly mastered. Progress keeps the week's streak, total XP and a word by word view, so the night before the test is an informed conversation rather than a guess.

  • Practise the tricky words in one tap
  • Recent lessons kept with stars and scores
  • Separate progress for each child

Ways to practise

Four ways to meet the same word.

Questions are mixed at random through the lesson, so the same ten words never feel like the same ten questions.

Unjumble

The letters are all there, in the wrong order. Good for noticing how a word is built.

Missing letters

Most of the word is shown. The gaps fall on the parts that are easy to get wrong.

Look, cover, write, check

The classroom method, on screen. Look at the word, cover it, build it from memory, check.

Listen and spell

Nothing on screen but the tiles. This is the one that decides whether a word is mastered.

Questions

The things parents ask first.

Who is it for?

Primary school children, roughly ages 5 to 11, from Reception to Year 6. It suits any child who gets a spelling list to learn at home.

Is there an app to install?

No. Spelling Nest opens in a browser on a phone, tablet or laptop. If you want it to feel like an app, use your browser's "Add to home screen" option and it will open full screen from an icon.

How long should my child play?

One lesson a day is plenty. A lesson is six to twelve questions and usually takes under ten minutes. Practice counts towards the streak on any day a lesson is finished.

How do this week's words get in?

A grown up types or pastes them into the Grown ups area. The list is cleaned up automatically, so you can paste straight from a message or a photo caption without tidying it first.

Does it follow the national curriculum?

Spelling Nest does not set the words. It practises the list your child's school sends home, so it matches whatever they are being taught that week. Every new profile starts with one example list so you can try it before your next list arrives.

Can I add more than one child?

Yes. Each child gets their own profile, their own word lists and their own progress, badges and streak, all under one grown up sign in.

Does my child need to type?

No. Every answer is given by tapping letter tiles. Spelling Nest never opens a keyboard for an answer, so spelling is tested rather than typing.

What happens when my child gets stuck?

On a Listen and spell question, Kit the owl steps in without being asked: first a slow replay with each letter group lighting up, then the first letter, then the number of letters and where the tricky part sits. Words that were helped still count for XP, but not towards mastery.

Does it work offline?

No. Spelling Nest needs an internet connection so that progress is saved to your account and is there on every device your family uses.

How do I sign in?

With your email address. We email you a code to type in, so there is no password to remember or reset. Your child never needs an email address of their own.

What does it cost?

Fourteen days free, then £3 a month or £20 for the year, which saves you £16. That covers every child in your family, not one child each.

Your card details are taken when you start, and nothing is charged for fourteen days. We email you before the first payment is due. Cancel at any point in those fourteen days and you pay nothing.

If you cancel later, practice carries on to the end of the period you have paid for. After that your word lists and progress stay exactly where they are and the lessons stop. Nothing is ever deleted.

How do I delete our data?

Open Grown ups and choose to delete all of your family's data. That removes the profiles, word lists and progress. You can also email info@spellingnest.co.uk to have your email address removed from the sign in system.
